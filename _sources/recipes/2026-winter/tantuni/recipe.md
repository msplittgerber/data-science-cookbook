# Tantuni

Tantuni is a famous street food from Mersin, a city on the Mediterranean coast of Turkey. Small strips of beef are first boiled and then fried in a wide, shallow pan with chili flakes, and finally wrapped in thin lavash bread together with tomatoes, onions, and parsley. It is quick, spicy, and juicy. For me, tantuni tastes like home: it is the first thing I eat whenever I am back in Turkey, and with this recipe I can bring a bit of that feeling to my kitchen in Kiel.

**Provided by:** Yaren Senöz

## Stats

- Cooking Time: 45 minutes (15 min prep, 30 min cooking)
- Servings: 4 wraps
- Difficulty: Easy
- Diet: Contains meat (see Variations for a vegetarian option)

## Ingredients

### For the Meat:

- 500 g Beef (e.g. topside or rump steak, not too lean)
- 1 tsp Salt (for boiling)
- 3 tbsp Sunflower oil (traditionally cottonseed oil)
- 1 tbsp Butter
- 1–2 tsp Turkish chili flakes (pul biber or isot)
- 1/2 tsp Ground cumin
- 1/2 tsp Salt
- 1/2 tsp Black pepper
- 2 tbsp Water

### For the Salad:

- 1 Red onion (thinly sliced)
- 1 tsp Sumac
- 1/2 bunch Flat-leaf parsley (finely chopped)
- 2 Tomatoes (finely diced)

### For Serving:

- 4 Thin lavash or durum flatbreads
- 1 Lemon (cut into wedges)
- Pickled chili peppers and ayran (salty yoghurt drink), optional

## Instructions

- **Step 1 – Cut the meat:** Cut the beef into very small, thin strips (about 1–2 cm long). Tip: put the meat in the freezer for 30 minutes before cutting; it becomes firm and is much easier to slice thinly.
- **Step 2 – Boil the meat:** Bring a pot of water to a boil, add 1 tsp of salt and the meat. Boil for about 10–15 minutes, then drain well. This step is what makes tantuni different from a normal stir-fry: the meat stays juicy and soft.
- **Step 3 – Prepare the salad:** Mix the onion slices with the sumac and a pinch of salt and rub them with your hands for a minute. Add the parsley. Keep the diced tomatoes separate.
- **Step 4 – Fry the meat:** Heat the oil and butter in a wide, shallow pan (a wok also works well) over high heat. Add the boiled meat and fry for 8–10 minutes until it starts to brown at the edges.
- **Step 5 – Season:** Add the chili flakes, cumin, salt, and black pepper. Stir and add 2 tbsp of water so that the spices form a light red, spicy sauce with the fat. Cook for 2 more minutes.
- **Step 6 – Warm the bread:** Lower the heat. Briefly press each flatbread into the pan so that it soaks up some of the spicy oil, then turn it and warm the other side for a few seconds.
- **Step 7 – Wrap:** Place a portion of meat on the bread, add tomatoes and the onion-parsley salad, squeeze some lemon juice over it, and roll it up tightly.
- **Step 8 – Serve:** Serve immediately with lemon wedges, pickled chili peppers, and a cold glass of ayran.

## Hints and Tricks

- **The bread trick is essential:** Dipping the lavash into the pan fat (Step 6) is what gives tantuni its typical taste. Do not skip it!
- **Do not overcook the meat in the pan:** It should be browned but still juicy. If it gets dry, add a little more water and butter.
- **Spiciness:** Isot (dark Urfa chili) gives a smoky taste; pul biber is fruitier. You can find both in Turkish supermarkets in Kiel.
- **Leftovers:** The boiled meat can be kept in the fridge for 2 days, so you can boil a bigger batch and fry fresh portions whenever you want.

## Variations
- **Chicken tantuni:** Use 500 g chicken thighs instead of beef. Skip the boiling step and fry the chicken strips directly for about 10 minutes.
- **Vegetarian tantuni:** Use 500 g mushrooms (cut into strips) or crumbled firm tofu. Fry until golden and season the same way.
- **Tantuni bowl:** Instead of wrapping, serve the meat and salad on a plate with bulgur pilaf or rice.
